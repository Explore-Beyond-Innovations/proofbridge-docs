---
title: "Run and recover a pod"
description: "Stop a pod in an emergency, rotate its settlement key without closing ads, and redeploy it with a new agent identity"
---

A **pod** is a running [agent](/v3/concepts/agents): a small program that
locks orders on your ads, within the on-chain policy you set on your agent
account. It holds two kinds of key, both created inside the pod:

- an **agent key**, which the agent account's policy allows to lock
  orders and do nothing else
- a **settlement key**, registered under your ad's settlement signer, which
  co-signs the trades the pod locks

Neither key can withdraw funds, close an ad or change the policy. Your
owner wallet keeps those powers, and its key never enters the pod.

This page is for makers and operators who need to stop, rotate or rebuild
a pod. To set one up, see [Set up an agent](/v3/guides/set-up-an-agent) and
[Hosted pods](/v3/guides/hosted-pods).

## Running your own or a hosted pod

| | Self-hosted | Hosted pod |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it runs | your own machine | infrastructure ProofBridge operates |
| Who holds the keys | you | the pod; only public keys leave it |
| RPC endpoints | yours | yours, entered on the request |
| Setup | you run the agent runtime and do the owner steps yourself | the setup page walks you through each step |
| Recovery | the steps on this page | mostly buttons on the agent page; the steps below explain what they do |

Either way, the same rules hold. The pod refuses to start without your own
RPC endpoints, and it can only lock within your policy. It also needs a
registered settlement key and a filed emergency stop before it goes live.

## The kill levers

Three levers stop a pod. They stop different things, so pick the one that
matches what went wrong.

| Lever | What it stops | Reach for it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Retire the key** | the pod's settlement key, on every chain. It can no longer co-sign. | you want the pod's signatures to stop counting right away, whether or not the machine is still running |
| **Stop the pod** | the program itself. It stops locking and signing. | the machine is misbehaving, or you are done with it. The key stays registered until you retire it. |
| **Revoke the agent** | the agent key, on the agent account. It can never lock for that account again. | you think the agent key was copied or stolen. Revoking is instant and permanent. |

On a hosted pod, **Kill Agent** on the agent page retires the key on every
chain and then deletes the pod. **Revoke agent** is on the agent's
**Policy** tab.

When you revoke an agent, the **retirement service** also sends the
emergency stop you signed for its settlement key, so one action ends both
keys. You can retire the key yourself too: see
[Retire a key](/v3/guides/register-a-settlement-key#retire-a-key).

<Note>
A suspected compromise needs all three: revoke the agent, retire the key,
stop the pod. Revoking ends new locks. Retiring ends co-signing. Stopping
the pod ends the program. A stolen agent key can still only do what the
policy allows until you revoke it: lock within the caps, never withdraw.
</Note>

### What happens to open trades

Stopping or killing a pod doesn't touch the escrows. Each open trade ends
one of three ways:

- the pod already co-signed it: it completes when the Bridger signs
- the pod locked it but hadn't co-signed: you
  [take it over](/v3/guides/hosted-pods#take-over-a-deleted-pods-trades)
  and co-sign with your own key
- it was claimed or disputed already, or nobody finishes it: it ends
  through the [cancel path](/v3/concepts/cancels-and-refunds) at its
  deadline, and both deposits are refunded

## Rotate the settlement key without closing ads

Rotation replaces the pod's settlement key while its ads stay open and
keep trading. It works because a settlement signer can hold up to **five**
live keys at once, each in its own **slot** on the key registry of each
chain. See [Several keys at once](/v3/concepts/settlement-keys#several-keys-at-once).

When the pod locks an order, the lock **pins** the slot the order was
locked with. At payout, the ad chain checks the co-signature against that
pinned key and no other. That gives rotation its shape:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask the pod for a new key">
    The pod creates its next settlement key. On a hosted pod, start the
    rotation from the agent page (or
    [the API](/v3/api/pods#rotate)); the pod posts the new key within a
    minute. A self-hosted pod's runtime writes a registration request for
    the next key index.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register it and file its emergency stop">
    Sign one registration for the new key, which covers both chains, then
    sign its emergency stop. The new key needs its own stop: the old
    key's stop covers only the old slot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let new trades move over">
    New locks pin the new key once it's live on both chains. Orders locked
    before the rotation stay pinned to the old key and keep settling on it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Retire the old key">
    Retire the old key once its pinned orders have ended. The relayer stops
    accepting co-signatures under a key 15 minutes before its retirement
    time, so pick a time after the old key's open orders close.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If you need the old key gone sooner, an open order can be **re-pinned** to
a live key. A re-pin needs the settlement signer's consent, signed from the
order's current pin, and the trade then settles normally under the new
key. If the pinned key ends before the trade settles, both parties are
asked to sign again. A trade that still can't settle ends through the
cancel path, with both deposits refunded.

<Tip>
An ordinary redeploy or restart keeps the pod's identity. Its keys live on
its own storage, which survives redeploys. Rotation is always a deliberate
step, never a side effect.
</Tip>

## Redeploy a pod with a new agent identity

Use this when the pod's machine or keys may have been exposed. Rotating the
settlement key isn't enough then: the pod needs a **new agent key** too,
which means new key files, a new agent on your account, and a new
settlement key. Plan for about 10 minutes of your time, plus the time the
registrations take to land on both chains.

**On a hosted pod**, kill the agent and request a new pod:

1. On the agent's **Policy** tab, click **Revoke agent**.
2. Click **Kill Agent**: the key is retired on every chain, then the pod is
   deleted.
3. Take over any trades the old pod locked but hadn't co-signed (see
   [What happens to open trades](#what-happens-to-open-trades)).
4. On **My Agents → New Agent**, request a new hosted pod and follow
   [the steps](/v3/guides/hosted-pods#the-steps). The new pod creates fresh
   keys inside itself.

**On a self-hosted pod**, the same steps by hand:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Revoke the old agent">
    From your owner wallet, revoke the old agent key on the agent account,
    on each chain it trades on. Revoking also sends the old settlement
    key's emergency stop.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop the pod and clear its keys">
    Stop the runtime. Remove the contents of its key directory, or point it
    at a fresh, empty one. Keep the old files offline if you need them for
    an investigation, but never start a pod from them again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the pod with your RPC endpoints">
    Start the runtime with your own RPC endpoints for each chain. It
    refuses to start without them. On its first start it creates a new
    agent key and a new settlement key.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the new identity">
    The start-up log prints the pod's identity: its EVM address and its
    Stellar account. The pod also writes a registration request with the
    same public details. Nothing in it is secret.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fund the new accounts">
    The pod pays the network fee for each lock from its own account on each
    chain. Send gas to the new addresses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bind the new agent">
    From your owner wallet, set the agent policy on your agent account for
    the pod's new agent key, with your settlement signer named in it. On
    Stellar, if the ad has a guard rail, this change waits out the guard
    rail's delay first. Bind the new agent with the relayer too, so it is
    sent the trades.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register the new settlement key">
    Sign one registration for the pod's new settlement key, covering both
    chains. The relayer files both and pays the gas. If your agent account
    isn't yet an approved maker for your settlement signer, approve it now.
  </Step>
  <Step title="File the new emergency stop">
    Sign the emergency stop for the new key. The old stop doesn't cover the
    new slot, and the pod isn't ready to go live until this one is on file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go live">
    Keep the rebuilt pod in dry run until the key is live on both chains
    and the emergency stop is filed, then switch dry run off. The pod logs that
    its settlement key is registered, and starts locking within the policy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Clearing the key directory also clears the material the pod used to keep
its half of each trade secret. Open trades of the old pod that it hadn't
finished can't be finished by the new one. Take over the ones you can.
The rest end through the cancel path at their deadline, with both deposits
refunded.
</Warning>

## Limits

- One pod trades on one EVM chain. A second EVM route needs a second pod
  with its own identity.
- The agent key is stored on the pod's own storage. It can only lock within
  your policy and never withdraw, so a leak costs at most the policy's
  limits until you revoke it.
- ProofBridge can't retire your keys for you without the emergency stops
  you signed. File one for every key you register.
