A liquidity ad is the on-chain offer that makes peer-to-peer bridging
possible. When a Maker posts an ad, they commit liquidity in a specific
token on one chain against deposits of a specific token on another.
Bridgers pick the ad that matches their transfer.

## What a liquidity ad contains

- **The route:** which token a Bridger deposits, and which token (on which
  chain) the Maker provides. Tokens pair **1:1 by value**, less the
  [fee](/v3/concepts/fees); amounts are aligned across token decimals
  automatically ([decimal scaling](/v3/concepts/decimal-scaling)).
- **Pool amount:** the liquidity the Maker has escrowed
- **Min / max per trade:** optional limits
- **Settlement signer:** the wallet whose
  [settlement key](/v3/concepts/settlement-keys) co-signs the Maker's
  side of every trade. Usually the Maker's own wallet. If it's another
  wallet, that wallet has to approve the Maker first.
- **Ad ID:** the identifier orders and proofs reference

These live on-chain in the `AdManager` contract; the app's routes are
built from them.

The ad's maker can be a wallet or an [agent account](/v3/concepts/agents).
Only an ad held by an agent account can be served by an agent.

## Ad lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create + fund">
    The Maker submits `createAd` and escrows the pool. Only funded ads
    serve orders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reserved by an order">
    A Bridger creates an order against the ad, and the matching slice of
    the pool is **reserved** before any money moves. Reservations from
    orders that are never funded are released after ~30 minutes, so
    abandoned checkouts can't strand liquidity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Locked for the order">
    Once the Bridger's deposit confirms, the Maker locks the reserved
    liquidity on-chain (`lockForOrder`). This is the Maker's deposit into
    the trade. Both deposits are recorded in their chain's Merkle Mountain
    Range.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Co-signed">
    Both parties sign the settlement with their settlement keys. The trade
    can't settle without both signatures.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Settled, or ended">
    The payouts are proven and submitted on both chains. The locked amount
    leaves the ad, less the LP fee, which stays in the pool. If the trade
    doesn't settle by its deadline, the lock returns to the ad through the
    [cancel path](/v3/concepts/cancels-and-refunds).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Closed">
    The Maker can close the ad and reclaim the remaining pool once its
    trades have ended.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Available vs pool

The amount a new order can take is the on-chain pool **minus the holds of
open trades** (reservations and locks).

<Warning>
  Don't close an ad while trades are in progress: locked amounts stay
  held until their trades end.
</Warning>

## Pausing

When the contracts are paused, nothing new can start: no new ads, funding,
locks or orders. Everything already under way can still finish, settle,
cancel or be refunded, and no deadline is stretched.

## What prevents double-spends

Each payout consumes a **nullifier**, a one-time value bound to the trade
and the side it pays. Once it's recorded on-chain, any replay is rejected,
independently on each chain. Together with the co-signed roots and the
co-signed nullifiers, a deposit settles exactly once, in exactly one
direction, with both parties' consent.
