Phase 3 adds two capabilities held back from earlier phases:
**automated maker liquidity ops under a scoped policy**, and
**dispute paths for stuck or contested orders**. Both build on the
Phase 2 BLS foundation and introduce new on-chain primitives.

## How agents work

A maker who wants automated liquidity operations:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Deploy an Agent Account">
    Soroban: deploy a custom-account contract that implements
    `__check_auth`. EVM: deploy a per-maker Safe + Safe Module. Either
    way, the maker is the owner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the agent's policy">
    Owner calls `set_policy(agent_pubkey, policy)` with allowed actions,
    token whitelist, per-order cap, rate limit, expiry, and revocation
    flag.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register the agent's BLS pubkey">
    Owner registers the agent's BLS public key against the Agent
    Account's address. The agent becomes the maker's BLS-signing
    identity for trades within policy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point ads at the Agent Account">
    Maker creates ads with `ad_creator = agent_account_address`. The
    agent can call `lock_for_order` and produce BLS signatures within
    policy bounds — without ever holding the maker's wallet key.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate keys in one tx">
    To replace an agent (compromise, software change), the maker adds
    a new BLS key slot and sets `valid_until` on the old slot. No need
    to close ads or migrate liquidity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Agents cannot initiate disputes, modify their own policy, touch funds
outside the maker's ads, or operate beyond `valid_until` / rate-limit
ceilings. The protocol's own dispute agent — the **Auto-Arbiter** — holds
**no key and no role at all**: it assembles neutral case files for the
human arbiter and presents public evidence anyone could present.

## The dispute layer — the amended spec

This section is the published form of the committed design's §3, as
amended 2026-08-27 (amendment blocks **A1–A7**). The one-line summary:
**evidence first, arbiter second, timeout last — and every path lands on
the same primitive.**

### Where disputes live

A dispute files on the **AdManager leg only** — the ad leg is the
dispute's head. `dispute(order, evidence_hash)` is **party-gated** (only
the order's maker or its named recipient may file; anyone else could
otherwise dispute-and-cancel a live order out from under both parties)
and posts the bond with the filing. The OrderPortal leg has no dispute
surface, no arbiter, and no dispute clock: the follower leg learns the
outcome by **proof** — a leaf-inclusion proof against the counterpart
chain's anchored root, using the event circuit's leaf domains
(cancel = 2, settled = 3, forfeit = 5).

### States

```text
None → Open → Filled                        (happy path, unchanged)
       Open → Cancelled                     (provable-first unwind after deadline)
       Open → Disputed → Filled             (evidence terminates the dispute)
       Open → Disputed → Resolved           (arbiter ruling or timeout default)
```

The two `Disputed` exits differ deliberately (A6): the **evidence path**
runs the real settlement and terminates to `Filled`; the **arbiter and
timeout paths** publish no proof and terminate to `Resolved`.

### A1 — one termination primitive

`cancel_order` / `cancel_lock` no longer finalize atomically. Both are
entry points to a single `resolve_by_evidence` primitive:
**claim → presentation window → finalize**. Presented evidence (the
co-signed settlement package, or a counterpart-settlement-leaf proof)
settles the leg; absence of evidence cancels and refunds. The AdManager
leg's clock is the only cancel clock; the follower's refund is
proof-gated on the primary's cancellation leaf. **No automatic
finalization, from any entry state, completes before
`deadline + BUFFER`** (verified: T-50).

### A2 + A5 — evidence supremacy

Presented settlement evidence terminates a `Disputed` order to
`TradeProceeds` **at any time** — not only at the challenge deadline. A
dispute can delay a provable settlement only by presentation latency.

The old framing — *"disputes override the protocol's normal auth
checks"* — is replaced outright: **protocol evidence overrides
arbitration, never the reverse.** The arbiter fills the evidence vacuum
and cannot contradict evidence, execute `TradeProceeds`, manufacture a
dispute, or act outside `Disputed` orders.

One consequence, verified live in CI: **the co-signed unlock is itself
evidence.** A disputed order's unlock path admits `Disputed` and closes
the dispute in the same transaction — a filer cannot use a bond to hold
a fully co-signed settlement hostage even for one presentation round.

### A3 — the vacuum-only arbiter

- `TradeProceeds` is **evidence-exclusive** — only the evidence
  primitive executes it. The arbiter's menu is `{MutualRefund,
  BridgerForfeit, MakerForfeit}`.
- A ruling is **propose-then-window**: it opens the presentation window
  and pays nothing. Presented evidence overrides a standing ruling
  (verified: T-51, and live in the e2e suite). Subjective rulings stand
  only in the evidence vacuum.
- All outcome payouts and bond transfers route through the `claimable`
  **pull-escrow** when a direct payout is refused — a reverting
  recipient cannot brick resolution (verified: T-18).

### The timeout default

If no arbiter ever acts, anyone may call the permissionless
`claim_dispute` after the challenge period (on-chain minimum: **1 hour**,
no local carve-outs), which opens the same presentation window; finalize
then applies the unresolved default, which unwinds like a cancel. Every
stranded path resolves through the provable-first primitive to the
*correct* outcome — settle if evidence exists, refund otherwise.

### A4 + A7 — the bond

**Sizing (A7):** `bond = max(floor, X% × order value)` — `floor` and `X`
are admin-set per route, computed at filing (one call site). Value
scaling removes the free-cancel exposure a fixed bond would leave on
large orders; the floor keeps small-order disputes non-free.

**Routing** — the destination follows one question: *was the filer shown
wrong?*

| Termination path | Bond destination |
|---|---|
| **Evidence terminates the dispute** *(A4, new row)* | forfeited to `protocol_fee_pool` — the filer disputed a provably-settleable trade |
| Ruling: `MutualRefund` | returned to the filer |
| Ruling against the counterparty | returned to the filer |
| Ruling against the filer | forfeited to `protocol_fee_pool` |
| Timeout default (no arbiter ever acted) | returned to the filer — nobody was shown wrong |

The routing table is enforced identically on both chains by the T-24
differential suite; the timeout row was pinned empirically by that
suite — both implementations agree.

### A6 — the nullifier resolves by path

The evidence path runs the real unlock and consumes the nullifier
naturally. The arbiter path publishes no proof, so no nullifier exists
to consume — the terminal `Resolved` status is the replay guard.

### The four outcomes

`DisputeOutcome` covers the four real-world resolutions. Outcomes
execute on the ad leg (the dispute's host); the order leg converges via
leaf proofs against the anchored root:

| Outcome | Source side (OrderPortal) | Destination side (AdManager) |
|---|---|---|
| `MutualRefund` | Bridger refunded | `ad.locked -= ad_amount` (back into ad balance) |
| `TradeProceeds` *(evidence-exclusive)* | Bridger's deposit → maker's recipient | Maker's locked balance → bridger's recipient |
| `BridgerForfeit` | Bridger's deposit → maker (forfeit) | `ad.locked -= ad_amount` |
| `MakerForfeit` | Bridger refunded | Maker's locked balance → bridger's recipient |

### How this is verified

- **T-21** — evidence executes `TradeProceeds` with no arbiter
  signature; none → refund; the Auto-Arbiter holds no key.
- **T-24** — the cross-implementation differential: one scenario
  fixture, two hand-written implementations (Foundry + Soroban), one
  required terminal record per scenario — status, appended leaf domain,
  and bond destination. A divergence fails a named test instead of
  surfacing as a cross-chain incident.
- **T-50** — no finalization before `deadline + BUFFER` from any entry
  state.
- **T-51 / T-54 / T-55** — evidence supremacy over standing rulings,
  immediate termination + bond routing, partial-ruling convergence.
- **T-18** — a reverting recipient cannot brick resolution.
- **Live e2e** — the relayer CI drives the full stack on real localnets:
  both trade directions file-then-settle with the dispute terminating
  through evidence, a standing ruling overridden by evidence, and the
  vacuum trio (batch file + rule, one warp, three finalizes) with exact
  bond routing.

## Stated boundaries: the fourteen residuals

These are **stated boundaries, not oversights**. Each was found by the
red-team pass, judged unclosable within T2's scope, and accepted
deliberately — carried here verbatim so a reviewer reads a declared
boundary, not an unnoticed gap. (Deep-dive links resolve in the
[engineering record](https://github.com/Explore-Beyond-Innovations/ProofBridge/tree/main/docs/research/t2)
— `docs/research/t2/` in the protocol repo.)

| # | Residual | Root | Closed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Cross-chain atomicity** — the two legs share no truth. **Re-scoped twice:** the *cancel* route closed by cancellation proofs (2026-08-27); the *dispute* route closed by the F2 composition — settlement leaf + evidence-termination. What remains: the buffered maker-unlock boundary (Tier-1, grace buffer) | epistemic gap (the atomicity deep dive) | remaining boundary: T3.5 listener, whose job has shrunk to **root notary** |
| 2 | **Withhold-to-refund** — co-signing is voluntary; a party can deny a provable settlement by not signing. **Team decision 2026-08-27: managed by reputation only** — counterparty fail-rate scoring (required runtime feature); on-chain pricing (unwind fee) evaluated and **not adopted**. The option stays free on-chain for one-shot accounts | consent-based settlement | priced further only by a later fee decision or single-sided attestation (unscoped) |
| 3 | **Single-admin arbiter** — **re-scoped 2026-08-27:** `TradeProceeds` is evidence-exclusive and rulings are propose-then-window, so the arbiter fills the evidence vacuum and can never contradict evidence | T2 ships admin-as-arbiter, now vacuum-bounded | T3 M-of-N (≥2 non-team), guarding a smaller power |
| 4 | **Bond ceiling** — **fixed in T2 (team decision 2026-08-27):** the value-scaled bond `max(floor, X%·value)` is the *mechanism*, not a recommendation (§3.4.1 amended, A7); it subsumes the T3 tier ladder | economics | closed — parameters re-tuned from soak data |
| 5 | **Owner-wallet compromise** — **re-scoped 2026-08-27:** opt-in extractive-action timelocks (protective actions never delayed) convert instant total loss to time-boxed flow; multisig custody for institutions. Instant-total-loss remains only for retail accounts declining guardrails, and for 7702 makers | custody boundary | guardrails in 2.1; T3 recovery/guardian schemes |
| 6 | **Price floor** — **retired as unclosable 2026-08-27:** `AgentPolicy.min_rate` per pair is the maker's own limit, oracle-free, enforced in `check_contract_call`; loss bounded by maker-chosen spread × caps | mis-framed as needing an oracle | residual: bound staleness, fails safe |
| 7 | **Keyless accounts can't register cross-chain** — the EOA identity routes around it. **Groundwork built in T2 (team decision 2026-08-27):** `Side.Registered = 4`, the home-chain registrar, and registry v2's flagged proof-accepting path; the flip waits for the anchor to be quorum-backed | epistemic gap (the keyless-registration deep dive) | flip = config change once 3.5's listener quorum writes anchors (T3) |
| 8 | **`target_root` authenticity is external** — two co-signatures are the only gate before escrow | epistemic gap (the root-authenticity deep dive) | zk light client / SCP proving (does not exist) |
| 9 | **`_fieldMod` canonicality** — **fixed in T2 (team decision 2026-08-27):** the escrow rejects non-canonical public inputs (`≥ PRIME`) on both chains, for both circuits — no verifier change needed (T-65) | pre-existing | closed |
| 10 | **Immutable accounts vs un-voted protocol** — P28's ADDRESS_V2/CAP-85 can't be fully tested before their votes; a miss = redeploy | Soroban deploy model | tracking (T-09); nothing structural |
| 11 | **Width narrowing** — **fixed in T2 (team decision 2026-08-27), riding the hash break:** `salt` becomes `U256` on Soroban; the `amount` narrowing becomes an explicit invariant on both chains (T-66) | type systems | closed |
| 12 | **Time-to-unwind is inherent** — *re-scoped 2026-08-27:* the backstop is no longer a blind timeout-cancel; every stranded path resolves through the provable-first primitive to the *correct* outcome (settle if evidence exists, refund otherwise). What remains inherent is the **time**: capital stays locked until `deadline + BUFFER` (plus proof/anchor latency on the follower leg) | consequence of 1 + 2 | same as 1 — the remaining window is the buffered boundary |
| 13 | **The RootAnchor trust tier** *(introduced by the design)* — refunds trust an admin-tier hot key to notarize counterpart-chain roots; hardened by the optimistic `ANCHOR_DELAY` window, key separation, and the provable-first backstop; fraud is self-documenting | our own cancellation-proof design | retires up the ladder: T3.5 listener quorum → light client |
| 14 | **Presentation liveness** *(introduced by the design)* — every timeout, dispute, and backstop finalization is safe only if **one holder of public evidence acts within the window**; the victim is always in that set. Universal silence degrades a co-signed trade to a symmetric unwind — finality loss, never theft | the presentation-window model | watchtower + Auto-Arbiter duties (T-47, T-61) reduce it; only consensus-verified state removes it |

Three of these — **1, 7, 8** — are the same underlying fact seen three
ways: the chains cannot observe each other. The full analysis (monotone
vs mutable facts, and why the remaining trust assumptions form a single
ladder of independent T3+ decisions) lives in the engineering record's
[residuals part](https://github.com/Explore-Beyond-Innovations/ProofBridge/blob/main/docs/research/t2/risks/residuals/index.md).

## Audit batch 2 — the scope statement

The second audit batch covers every fund-moving path this phase added or
changed:

1. `AgentPolicy.settlement_signer` + its lock-time policy check;
2. `Ad.settlementSigner` + the escrow consistency check, **on both
   chains** — new to Soroban, a changed pin on EVM;
3. `setSettlementSigner`, a new state-changing entry point;
4. the registry v2 slot machinery and its un-guarded retirement;
5. the dispute timeout-default — **unified with items 6–7 into the one
   `resolve_by_evidence` primitive**, so items 5–7 are one fund-moving
   path audited once;
6. **the cancellation-proof + funds-conservation set** — the event
   circuit (leaf domains: cancel = 2, settled = 3) + generated verifier,
   the cancellation- and settlement-leaf appends, the proof-gated refund
   path, the `RootAnchor` module, `claim` un-gated from pause, and the
   fund-bearing TTL rule; **and**
7. the proposed permissionless, contract-verified resolution path that
   agent auto-resolve depends on — the largest of the additions, and not
   yet part of the committed design.

No SCF action attaches to any of this: deliverables, success criteria,
dates, and budgets are unchanged — this page is the transparency channel.
