</Note>

Phase 3 adds two capabilities held back from earlier phases:
**automated maker liquidity operations under a scoped policy**, and
**a way for a stuck or contested order to end in the right place**. Both
build on the Phase 2 BLS foundation.

The dispute design in one line: **proof beats people.** A settlement that
can be proven always goes through, whatever anyone rules. The arbiter only
decides the cases where no such proof exists, and a dispute nobody rules on
ends in a mutual refund on its own clock.

## How agents work

A maker who wants automated liquidity operations:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Deploy an Agent Account">
    Soroban: deploy a custom-account contract that implements
    `__check_auth`. EVM: deploy the per-maker agent module. Either way,
    the maker is the owner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the agent's policy">
    Owner calls `set_policy(agent, policy)` with allowed actions (today
    only `lock_for_order`), token whitelist, per-order cap, rate limit,
    expiry, revocation flag, and the settlement signer the agent settles
    as.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register the agent's BLS key">
    Owner signs one message that registers the agent's BLS public key on
    both chains' registries. The agent becomes the maker's BLS-signing
    identity for trades within policy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point ads at the Agent Account">
    Maker creates ads whose settlement signer is the agent. The agent can
    call `lock_for_order` and co-sign settlements within policy bounds
    without ever holding the maker's wallet key.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate keys in one tx">
    To replace an agent (compromise, software change), the maker adds a
    new key slot and sets `valid_until` on the old one, or retires it.
    No need to close ads or migrate liquidity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Agents cannot file disputes, modify their own policy, touch funds outside
the maker's ads, or operate beyond `valid_until` or the rate-limit ceiling.

## How disputes work

### The states

The order lifecycle on the primary leg (the `AdManager`, on the ad chain):

```text
None → Open → Filled                              happy path: the co-signed unlock
       Open → Claimed → Cancelled                  the termination primitive at the deadline
       Open → Disputed → Filled                    evidence landed: TradeProceeds
       Open → Disputed → Resolved                  a ruling, or the no-ruling fallback
```

Only the primary has a dispute. The follower leg (the `OrderPortal`, on the
order chain) has no dispute surface at all. It learns how a dispute ended
from the leaf the primary writes into its Merkle Mountain Range, and acts on
that leaf alone, the same way it learns every other cross-chain fact.

### Filing and the bond

Either party to an order can file a dispute on the primary while the order
is `Open`, before the route's deadline. Filing does three things and
nothing more: it posts a bond, it stops the cancel clock on the primary,
and it hands the dispute to the dispute module, which keeps the record and
the money. Filing pays nobody and decides nothing. The counterparty may
`respondToDispute`, which records an evidence hash on the dispute; it moves
no funds.

The bond is wrapped native, sized **`max(floor, bps × order amount)`**, both
parameters admin-set per peer chain and validated fail-closed:
`challengePeriod` is at least one hour, `bondFloor` is non-zero, `bondBps`
is at most 1000 (10%). Scaling the bond with the order removes the
free-cancel exposure a flat bond would leave on large orders; the
parameters are re-tuned from soak data.

### Evidence beats any ruling

**Protocol evidence overrides arbitration, never the reverse.** Settlement
evidence is one of two things:
the co-signed unlock itself, or a ZK proof that the counterparty's leg
settled, carried across as a leaf in the other chain's MMR.

Presented evidence terminates a `Disputed` order to `TradeProceeds` **at any
time**, not only at the challenge deadline. The order
ends `Filled`, the real unlock runs, and the nullifier is consumed as in the
happy path. A dispute can delay a provable settlement only by the time it
takes someone to present the proof.

The arbiter fills the evidence vacuum and cannot contradict evidence. It
cannot execute `TradeProceeds`, manufacture a dispute, or act on an order
that is not `Disputed`. A compromised agent is handled by key retirement,
policy caps and the maker's `min_rate`, not by clawing back a provably
authorized settlement.

### The arbiter rules the vacuum

A ruling is **propose-then-window**. `resolveDispute`
records one of `MutualRefund`, `BridgerForfeit` or `MakerForfeit` and opens
a presentation window ending at `max(now, deadline) + buffer`. Inside that
window the losing side can still present settlement evidence, which
overrides the ruling. After it, `finalizeDispute` applies the ruling and
moves the order to `Resolved`. `finalizeDispute` is permissionless; the
relayer's janitor sends it on the parties' behalf, and anyone may.

If nobody rules, the dispute does not hang. Once the challenge deadline
passes, anyone can finalize it and the outcome is `MutualRefund`. Nothing
depends on the arbiter ever showing up. The deadline respects both clocks
(the challenge period and the order's own window) and every pause since the
order was locked, because a paused escrow stops the parties from presenting
evidence.

No automatic finalization, from either entry state, completes before
`deadline + buffer`.

### The four outcomes

| Outcome | How it arises | Primary leg (`AdManager`) | Leaf written | Follower leg (`OrderPortal`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `TradeProceeds` | evidence only, never a ruling | the co-signed unlock or `presentSettled` pays the bridger; order `Filled` | `SETTLED` | `presentSettled` if the maker's leg is not already `Filled` |
| `MutualRefund` | a ruling, or no ruling by the deadline | `ad.locked −= amount`; the lock returns to ad liquidity; `Resolved` | `CANCEL` | `refundByCancel`: the bridger is refunded |
| `BridgerForfeit` | a ruling | `ad.locked −= amount`; the lock returns to the maker's liquidity; `Resolved` | `FORFEIT` | `payMakerByForfeit`: the bridger's deposit goes to the maker |
| `MakerForfeit` | a ruling | the maker's stake is paid to the order recipient; `Resolved` | `CANCEL` | `refundByCancel`: the bridger ends up with both legs |

Every ruling that means "refund the bridger" reuses the cancel path the
follower already had. Only a bridger forfeit needed a leaf domain of its
own, because handing the follower a `CANCEL` proof for a forfeit would be a
proof of the opposite ruling. The arbiter does not "issue matching outcomes
on both chains": it rules once, on the primary, and the follower follows
the leaf.

### Where the bond goes

One rule: the bond returns to the filer only when the finding was on their
side.

| How the dispute ends | Bond destination |
|---|---|
| Evidence terminates it (`TradeProceeds`) | forfeited to the protocol fee pool: the filer disputed a provably settleable trade |
| `MutualRefund` (ruling or fallback) | returned to the filer: neither side was shown wrong |
| A forfeit ruling against the party the filer accused | returned to the filer |
| A forfeit ruling against the filer | forfeited to the protocol fee pool |

All outcome payouts and the forfeited bond route through the pull-payment
escrow; the fee pool is a credit, never a push transfer, so a reverting
recipient cannot block resolution. An unset fee pool sends the bond back to
the filer rather than stranding it.

### The nullifier resolves by path

The **evidence path** runs the real unlock and consumes the nullifier
naturally. The **arbiter path** publishes no proof, so no nullifier exists
to consume; the terminal `Resolved` status is the replay guard.

### What the relayer does, and does not do

The relayer holds no dispute authority and cannot move funds on its own.
Its dispute watch asks the first question whenever a dispute is filed: did
the trade settle on the other chain? If so it presents that proof, which
ends the dispute without a ruling. Otherwise it assembles a tamper-evident
case file for the human arbiter before the challenge deadline, and pages
when a dispute is about to expire unresolved. Its only writes are the
ordinary, permissionless, proof-carrying calls (`finalizeDispute`,
`presentSettled`, `refundByCancel`, `payMakerByForfeit`) that anyone could
send. If the whole relayer were down, the parties could still present their
own evidence and the arbiter could still rule.

## Using the BLS auth circuit in disputes

The standalone Noir BLS auth circuit from
[Phase 2](/architecture/phase-2-bls-auth) lets a party prove "I held a
valid aggregated BLS authorization for this trade" without publishing the
raw aggregated signature. The main evidence in a dispute is the settlement
leaf itself, verified on-chain by the `events` circuit; the auth proof
remains available as responder evidence.

## Known boundaries

The risk analysis behind this design found forty-one issues across three
surfaces. Fourteen cannot be closed within Tranche 2 and are accepted
deliberately. They are **stated boundaries, not oversights**, and this page
is where they are published, so that a reviewer reads a declared boundary
rather than an unnoticed gap. The deep dives are linked under Sources.

| # | Boundary | What remains | What closes the rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cross-chain atomicity**: the two legs share no truth. The cancel route is closed by cancellation proofs; the dispute route by the settlement leaf plus evidence termination | the buffered maker-unlock boundary (the grace buffer) | the T3.5 listener, whose job is root notary |
| 2 | **Withhold-to-refund**: co-signing is voluntary, so a party can deny a provable settlement by not signing | managed by reputation (the counterparty fail-rate scoring); free on-chain for one-shot accounts | a later fee decision or single-sided attestation (unscoped) |
| 3 | **Single-admin arbiter**: T2 ships admin-as-arbiter | the arbiter fills the evidence vacuum only and can never contradict evidence (`TradeProceeds` is evidence-exclusive; rulings open a window) | T3 M-of-N (at least 2 non-team), guarding a smaller power |
| 4 | **Bond ceiling** | closed: the value-scaled bond `max(floor, X%·value)` is the mechanism | parameters re-tuned from soak data |
| 5 | **Owner-wallet compromise** | opt-in extractive-action timelocks (protective actions never delayed) turn instant total loss into a time-boxed flow; multisig custody for institutions; instant loss remains for retail accounts declining guardrails and for 7702 makers | T3 recovery and guardian schemes |
| 6 | **Price floor**: not an oracle problem | `AgentPolicy.min_rate` per pair is the maker's own limit, enforced in `check_contract_call`; loss is bounded by the maker-chosen spread × caps | bound staleness; fails safe |
| 7 | **Keyless accounts can't register cross-chain** | the EOA identity routes around it; the proof-carried registration path is built behind a flag (`Side.Registered`, the home-chain registrar) | the flag flips once the anchor is quorum-backed (T3) |
| 8 | **`target_root` authenticity is external** | two co-signatures are the only gate before escrow | a zk light client or SCP proving (does not exist today) |
| 9 | **`_fieldMod` canonicality** | closed: the escrow rejects non-canonical public inputs (at or above the field prime) on both chains, for both circuits | — |
| 10 | **Immutable accounts vs un-voted protocol**: Protocol 28's ADDRESS_V2 / CAP-85 can't be fully tested before their votes | a miss means a redeploy | tracking only; nothing structural |
| 11 | **Width narrowing** | closed: `salt` is `U256` on Soroban; the `amount` narrowing is an explicit invariant on both chains | — |
| 12 | **Time-to-unwind is inherent** | every stranded path resolves through the provable-first primitive to the correct outcome (settle if evidence exists, refund otherwise); what remains is the **time**: capital stays locked until `deadline + buffer`, plus proof and anchor latency on the follower leg | same as 1 |
| 13 | **The RootAnchor trust tier**: refunds trust an admin-tier hot key to notarize counterpart-chain roots | hardened by the optimistic `ANCHOR_DELAY` window, key separation and the provable-first backstop; fraud is self-documenting | retires up the ladder: T3.5 listener quorum, then a light client |
| 14 | **Presentation liveness**: every timeout, dispute and backstop finalization is safe only if one holder of public evidence acts within the window | the victim is always in that set; universal silence degrades a co-signed trade to a symmetric unwind: finality loss, never theft | watchtower and auto-arbiter duties reduce it; only consensus-verified state removes it |

Three of these (1, 7, 8) are not independent. They are the same fact seen
three ways: the chains cannot observe each other. ProofBridge's constitution
is that no committee, no oracle and no messaging layer sits between the
chains; the only thing that crosses is a ZK proof plus a BLS co-signature,
data any party can carry.

## What the second external audit covers

The on-chain code that is new in Tranche 2, handed to audit batch 2 as one
list:

1. `AgentPolicy.settlement_signer` and its lock-time policy check;
2. `Ad.settlementSigner` and the escrow consistency check, on both chains;
3. `setSettlementSigner`, a new state-changing entry point;
4. the registry v2 slot machinery and its retirement path;
5. the `resolve_by_evidence` primitive: the timeout default, the
   cancellation and settlement leaves, the proof-gated refund path, the
   `RootAnchor` module, `claim` un-gated from pause, and the fund-bearing
   TTL rule, together with the `events` circuit (leaf domains: cancel = 2,
   settled = 3) and its generated verifier, audited once as one
   fund-moving path;
6. the permissionless, contract-verified resolution path the relayer's
   dispute watch depends on.
